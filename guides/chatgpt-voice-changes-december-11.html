ChatGPT Advanced Voice Ends on 11 December: What Changes If You Use Voice for Work

If you talk to ChatGPT rather than type, the voice you use is changing. On 11 December, Advanced and Standard voice retire and Live voice takes over. Here's what that means, and how to make voice useful at work before then.

If you use ChatGPT advanced voice to think out loud on the drive in, rehearse a presentation or talk through a tricky email, the voice you're using is about to change. OpenAI is retiring Advanced voice and Standard voice on 11 December 2026. After that date, every voice conversation in ChatGPT uses Live voice.

Nothing breaks today. You can keep choosing the older voices until the retirement date. But it's worth knowing what's different before the switch happens in the middle of something you care about.

What's changing

OpenAI's help article sets out the timeline. Users were notified from 10 November. Until 11 December you can still pick Advanced or Standard voice. On 11 December those options disappear from the voice picker in Settings > Voice, and anyone who had chosen them is moved to Live voice automatically.

Live voice runs on a model OpenAI calls GPT-Live, which it introduced in July. OpenAI says virtually everyone using voice in ChatGPT has already moved to it, so you may be using it without realising.

What's different about Live voice

The older Advanced voice waited for you to stop speaking before it answered. That's why conversations with it felt like taking turns on a two-way radio.

Live voice can listen and speak at the same time. OpenAI says it can show it's following with a quick "mhmm", handle fast back-and-forth, or stay quiet while you think. It can also hand a harder question to ChatGPT's text models and keep talking with you while that runs in the background.

In practice, that means you can interrupt it, change direction mid-sentence, and pause to think without it jumping in.

What stays the same

Your old voice chats. Conversations you had with Advanced or Standard voice stay in your ChatGPT history. You can carry on with them by typing or by voice.

Read Aloud. The option that reads a written answer out to you isn't part of this change.

Live video and screen sharing. These keep using Advanced voice for now, until GPT-Live can do them. OpenAI hasn't given a date.

Custom GPTs. Voice in a custom GPT keeps using Advanced voice until custom GPTs themselves retire, which is also scheduled for 11 December.

OpenAI's notice is about voice conversations. It doesn't mention dictation, the microphone that turns what you say into typed text in the message box.

Three ways to use voice at work

Voice is most useful for work that starts in your head rather than in a document.

Rehearse before it matters. Practise a presentation, a pitch or a hard conversation out loud, and ask for honest feedback. Because Live voice copes with interruptions, you can stop, restart a section and ask "was that clearer?" without starting again.

I'm going to rehearse a five-minute update for my leadership team about [topic]. Listen without interrupting until I say "done". Then tell me: - the one point that came across most clearly - anything I said that was vague or would invite a hard question - what to cut if I only had three minutes Be direct. Then ask me to try the opening again.

Think out loud, get it in writing. Talk through a problem on a walk, then ask for a written summary at the end. The transcript and the summary stay in the chat, so you can pick it up at your desk.

Hand a task over by voice. If your plan includes ChatGPT Work, you can start a Live conversation inside Work and ask it to draft a document or do some research while you talk. OpenAI says that on web and mobile you open ChatGPT, choose Work, then select the Voice control. Anything that needs your approval still has to be approved on screen, because spoken approval isn't supported. Our guide to ChatGPT scheduled tasks shows another way to have work done for you without asking each time.

💡 Voice doesn't change your privacy rules

Speaking to ChatGPT is the same as typing to it. If you wouldn't paste a client's details into a personal account, don't say them out loud to one either. And remember who can hear you: a rehearsal of a restructure announcement on a crowded train is a different kind of leak.

Before 11 December

If you've stuck with Advanced voice because you liked its style, try Live voice now on something low-stakes, while you can still switch back. If the answers feel thinner than you're used to, ask for the detail directly: "Give me the full version, with the three steps spelled out." OpenAI says it's still improving Live voice's depth and detail.

If you use voice inside a custom GPT, plan for that GPT retiring on the same day. Its replacement will use Live voice.

Try it this week

Open ChatGPT, go to Settings > Voice and check which voice you're using. Then pick one conversation you have coming up, a presentation or a difficult call, and rehearse it once with the prompt above. Ten minutes out loud will tell you more than an hour of rereading your notes.
