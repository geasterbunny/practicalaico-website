Copilot Says You've Hit a Limit: What It Means and What to Do

If Copilot told you to come back later, it probably isn't broken. Microsoft has started limiting Copilot Chat for people without a paid licence. Here's what's changed and how to work within it.

You ask Copilot a few questions, and then a notice appears saying you've reached your limit and should try again later. Nothing else on your computer has changed. It's natural to assume something is broken.

In most cases it isn't. This month Microsoft began applying fair-use limits to Copilot Chat for people at work who don't have a paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licence. The Register reported that the limits reached organisations with 2,000 seats or fewer from 2 November, and larger ones from 9 November.

First, work out which Copilot you have

There are two versions of Copilot at work, and they look similar.

Copilot Chat comes with your Microsoft 365 work account at no extra cost. It's the chat window at m365.cloud.microsoft/chat or in the Copilot app. It answers questions and helps you write, but it doesn't have full access to your email, meetings and files.

Microsoft 365 Copilot is the paid licence your employer buys per person. It adds Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, and lets it work with your own documents and mail.

To check, open the Copilot app and look for a Work and Web toggle near the top of the chat. If you can see Work, you most likely have the paid licence. If you're not sure, ask IT. They can tell you in one line.

What the limit is

The frustrating part is that Microsoft hasn't published the numbers. According to The Register's report, the limits aren't listed anywhere and your IT administrator can't raise them for you. The notice tells you when you can try again, and it may include an option to request a licence.

That means you can't plan around an exact figure. You can only use your chats more carefully and decide whether your work justifies a licence.

What to do when you see the notice

Read the retry time. The notice says when Copilot will be available again. Restarting, signing out or clearing your browser won't bring it back sooner.

Check there isn't an outage. If Copilot won't load at all, rather than showing a limit notice, look at status.cloud.microsoft before assuming it's the limit.

Don't switch to a personal AI account for confidential work. The limit is annoying, but it doesn't change what you're allowed to paste into tools your employer hasn't approved.

Make each chat go further

Most people use far more messages than they need, because they ask in small pieces. One complete request does the work of five short ones.

I need a reply to the email below. About me: I'm the [your role] at [organisation]. The sender is [who they are]. What I want: a polite reply that [agrees to X / declines Y / asks for Z]. Tone: friendly and brief, no more than 120 words. Must include: [date, amount, or next step]. Give me one version. Underneath, list anything you had to assume. [paste the email]

Three more habits help:

Batch small jobs. Ask for the three subject lines, the summary and the follow-up in a single message.

Edit small things yourself. If one sentence is wrong, fix it by hand. Asking for a rewrite spends a message and usually changes things you liked.

Save what works. Keep your best prompts in a document so you don't spend messages rebuilding them.

💡 Copilot button missing from Word or Excel?

That's usually a licence question too, not a fault. Check you're signed in to Office with your work account, not a personal one, and that your apps are up to date. If the button is still missing, ask IT whether you have the paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licence, because the in-app Copilot depends on it in many organisations.

When to ask for a licence

A licence costs your employer money each month, so a request goes further with evidence. For one week, note each time you hit the limit and what you were trying to do. If it's stopping work that matters, such as summarising long email threads or drafting reports from your own files, send your manager three lines: what you use Copilot for, how often you're blocked, and roughly how much time it would save. That's a much stronger case than "Copilot keeps cutting out".

Try it this week

Find out which Copilot you have by looking for the Work and Web toggle or asking IT. Then use the prompt above for your next email reply and count how many messages it took. If you still hit the limit most days, start your one-week log.
