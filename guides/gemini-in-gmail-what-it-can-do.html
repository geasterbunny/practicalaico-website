Gemini in Gmail: What It Can Do for Your Inbox (and What's Still US-Only)

Gemini in Gmail can summarise long threads, draft replies, find details buried in old emails and tidy your inbox, with a confirmation step before it changes anything. Here's how to open it, what to ask, and which new features haven't reached Australia yet.

What Gemini in Gmail is

Gemini in Gmail is Google's AI assistant built into your inbox. Instead of copying an email into a separate chatbot, you ask questions about your email where it already lives. It can read the thread you have open, search the rest of your mailbox, and draw on your Google Drive and Calendar when you ask it to.

You need an eligible plan: a Google Workspace plan that includes Gemini (most business plans do) or a paid Google AI plan. If you use Gmail through work and can't see it, your administrator may have switched it off.

How to open it

On your computer, open Gmail and click Ask Gemini at the top right. A panel opens on the right with a prompt box at the bottom. Type your request and click Submit.

A few controls worth knowing: you can switch between a Side and Wide view, copy a response with one click, and add a Google Drive file as a source. Type @ in the prompt box to pick one from a list. Your conversation history sits behind the menu at the top left.

Five things worth asking it

1. Summarise a long thread. Open the thread and ask for the short version. This is the one most people use every day.

Summarise this thread in five bullet points. Then list any action items, who owns each one, and any deadlines mentioned. Finish with anything that is still unresolved or waiting on me.

2. Draft or improve a reply. Ask it to draft a response, or paste your own draft and ask it to make it clearer or more polite. Tell it what you want to say. It writes better when it knows your position, not just the topic.

Draft a reply to this email. My position: we can deliver by the 14th, not the 7th, because the supplier is late. Tone: friendly and direct, no grovelling. Keep it under 120 words and end with a clear next step.

3. Find a detail you know is in there somewhere. "When did the supplier confirm the new price?" or "Find the email from Sarah about the venue booking last month." It's often faster than guessing at search terms.

4. Propose meeting times. Gemini can suggest times based on your Google Calendar availability. It can only create events on your primary calendar, so check where the booking lands.

5. Tidy your inbox. You can ask it to archive, delete, label, star or mark emails as read, for example "Archive all the automated report emails from last week." It asks you to confirm each action, and you have 60 seconds to undo it afterwards.

💡 Read before you send, confirm before you clean

Gemini drafts. You send. Read every draft for the facts it can't know: what you agreed on the phone, what the client is sensitive about, what your manager wants kept quiet. And when it offers to archive or delete a batch of emails, read the list before you confirm. A clean-up you didn't check is how an important email disappears.

What's new, and what's US-only for now

Google has announced two more Gmail features this month for paid Workspace plans. AI Inbox pulls suggested to-dos and topic summaries out of your email threads. Gmail Live lets you ask questions about your inbox out loud on your phone and hear the answer.

Both are currently limited to users in the US with their language set to English (US). If you're in Australia and don't see them, nothing is wrong with your account. They haven't reached you yet.

What it doesn't replace

Gemini is good at reading and drafting. It's not good at judgement. It doesn't know which client is about to walk, which colleague needs a softer tone, or that "let's discuss" from your CEO means "this is a problem". Use it to get through volume faster, and keep the decisions for yourself.

If your organisation has a policy on AI use, it applies here too. Before you point Gemini at sensitive HR, legal or customer emails, check what your workplace allows.

Try it this week

The next time you open a thread with more than ten replies, don't scroll. Click Ask Gemini and paste the first prompt above. Compare its action items with what you would have pulled out yourself. If it gets them right three times in a row, make it the first thing you do with every long thread.
