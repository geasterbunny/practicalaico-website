How to Make a NotebookLM Slide Deck From Your Own Documents

NotebookLM is now called Gemini Notebook, and its slide decks just got more control: you can steer the format, revise slide by slide, and on some plans review the outline before anything is built. Here's how to get a deck you'd actually present.

First, the name change

If you search for "NotebookLM slide deck" and can't find NotebookLM, that's because Google renamed it Gemini Notebook in July. It's the same tool: you add your own sources (reports, PDFs, Google Docs, web pages) and it answers questions and builds things using only those sources. Your existing notebooks and shared links still work.

That "only your sources" part is why it's useful for work decks. A general chatbot fills gaps with whatever it thinks sounds right. A notebook deck is built from the documents you gave it, which makes it much easier to check.

How to make a slide deck

1. Build the notebook. Open Gemini Notebook, create a new notebook and add the documents the presentation should be based on. For a quarterly update, that might be the Q3 report, the budget summary and last quarter's deck. Fewer, better sources beat a pile of loosely related files.

2. Open Slide Deck. In the Studio panel, select Slide Deck. You can also ask for one in the chat, and Gemini Notebook will offer you option cards to adjust.

3. Customise before you generate. Choose the format, the output language, the length and which sources to use. Then describe the deck you want in plain words. This description does most of the work, so be specific about audience, purpose and tone.

Create a deck for the leadership team summarising our Q3 results. Audience: senior managers who have five minutes and want the headline first. Structure: one slide on the overall result, one on what went well, one on what didn't, one on the risks for Q4, and one slide with the three decisions we need from them. Style: plain and professional, one idea per slide, short headings, no jargon. Use only the Q3 report and the budget summary as sources.

4. Generate. Select Generate now, or Generate later if you want to queue it. It can take several minutes, and you can keep working in the notebook while it runs.

Review the outline first (if your plan has it)

The newest addition is a review step. Before Gemini Notebook designs any slides, it shows you the outline and lets you give feedback. Fixing the structure at this stage is far quicker than fixing a finished deck.

To use it, turn on Review outline notes before you select Generate now. When the outline is ready, select Review next to it in Studio, write all your feedback in one go, then select Proceed. After you proceed you can't refine the outline any further, so say everything you need the first time.

One catch: Google's help page currently lists outline review for Google AI Ultra subscribers, while its rollout announcement describes a wider release. If you don't see the toggle, your account doesn't have it yet. Everything else in this guide still works.

Fix it slide by slide

Once the deck exists, don't start again from scratch to fix one slide. Select the deck in Studio, select Revise at the top, and type an instruction on each slide that needs changing: "shorten this heading", "swap this chart for a simple table", "move this slide to the end". You can also delete slides and drag thumbnails to reorder them. Then select Generate revised deck.

Two things to know about revisions. Each revision creates a new deck, and there's a limit on how many decks you can generate, so batch all your changes into one round. And revisions don't look at your sources again, so if a number is wrong, fix it by telling it the correct figure rather than asking it to "check the report".

💡 Check before you present

Google's own help page warns that slide decks "may contain visual or factual inaccuracies." Before the deck goes anywhere, open your source report next to it and check every number, date and name on every slide. It takes five minutes and it's the five minutes that protects you in the meeting.

Present, download or share

Select Start slideshow to present full screen straight from the notebook. To take the deck elsewhere, open the three-dots menu next to it and download it as a PDF or a PowerPoint (.pptx) file.

Do your fixing inside Gemini Notebook with Revise before you download. Don't plan on reworking the slides heavily in PowerPoint afterwards. Treat the download as close to finished.

If you share a link instead, check who can see it. Sharing a deck by link needs the notebook itself to be shared, and public links are switched off for Workspace Enterprise and Education accounts. Deleting a deck breaks any link to it. You also need to be over 18 to use slide decks.

Where it works best

Notebook decks are strongest when the content already exists in documents: turning a 20-page report into a five-slide summary, a project plan into a kick-off deck, or training material into a short induction session. They're weaker when the deck needs your company template or complicated charts. For those, use the notebook deck as your outline and build the final version in your usual tool.

Try it this week

Pick one report you have to present or summarise this month. Put it in a new notebook, generate a five-slide deck using the prompt above, and do one round of revisions. Then check every figure against the report before you show anyone. You'll know in 20 minutes whether this belongs in your workflow.
