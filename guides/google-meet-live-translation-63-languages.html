Google Meet Live Translation: How to Use It on a Call With Someone Who Speaks Another Language

Meet can now translate speech and captions across 63 languages. Here's how to turn it on, how to speak so it works, and why to confirm the important parts in writing.

What Meet can do now

Google has added real-time speech translation to Google Meet. According to its October announcement, it translates both spoken audio and captions into each person's preferred language, across 63 languages in any combination, for one-on-one and team meetings. Any participant can turn it on for themselves, so you don't need the host to arrange anything.

Google's announcement didn't say which Workspace plans or regions get it, so check whether the option appears in your meetings before you promise a supplier a translated call.

Who it helps

It suits calls with an overseas supplier, a colleague whose first language isn't English, or a client who is more comfortable speaking their own. It is not a replacement for a qualified interpreter for contracts, legal discussions, medical information or anything where a misunderstanding has serious consequences.

How to set it up for yourself

1. Join the meeting. Open the meeting settings or the captions and language options once you're in. The exact menu names depend on your version of Meet, so look for the translation or caption language setting.

2. Pick your language. Choose the language you want to hear and read. Your colleague chooses theirs separately.

3. Test it in the first minute. Ask each person to say one short sentence and check the translation reads sensibly. Do this before anyone says anything that matters.

Speak so it translates well

Translation tools do better with clear, plain speech. Use short sentences. Avoid idioms and jokes ("let's touch base", "ballpark figure"), because they often translate literally. Say numbers, dates and names slowly, and repeat them. Pause between points so captions can catch up.

Before the call, write me a 6-line plain-language agenda for a meeting with a supplier whose first language is not English. Use short sentences, no idioms, and put every date, quantity and price in a table at the end. Topic: [your topic].

Confirm the important things in writing

After the call, send a short written summary of every decision, date and number, and ask the other person to confirm it. This catches any translation slips before they cost money. Our AI meeting notes workflow shows how to draft that follow-up quickly.

Also tell everyone that translation is running. If you are recording or taking notes as well, the usual consent rules still apply.

What to watch for

Names, numbers and technical terms are where translation errors cluster, as are words that mean different things in different industries. If a caption looks wrong, ask the speaker to say it a different way rather than assuming it's right. And remember that tone doesn't always carry over. A blunt translation can sound harsher than the speaker meant, so give the other person the benefit of the doubt.

If the call matters commercially, such as a price negotiation or a contract term, book a human interpreter or ask for the key points in writing. The live translation is there to keep the conversation moving.

If you run a regular call with the same overseas contact, agree a short glossary of the ten terms you use most (product names, units, abbreviations) and share it before the meeting. It makes both the translation and the human conversation smoother, and it takes ten minutes once.

This week's action

Before your next call with someone who speaks another language, send the plain-language agenda from the prompt above. In the meeting, run the one-sentence test, and afterwards send the written confirmation of every number and date.
