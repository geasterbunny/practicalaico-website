THE QUESTION NOBODY ANSWERED

Your website gets asked
things all night.

A café gets asked whether they cater for a group of twenty. A salon gets asked how long a full colour takes and whether there's anything free on Saturday. A cleaner gets asked if they do end-of-lease in Parramatta. A clinic front desk gets asked about parking and whether they open late on Wednesdays.

None of these are hard questions. You answer them a dozen times a week, usually by hand, usually late. And the person asking is comparing you with two or three other businesses in other tabs. Whoever answers clearly first tends to get the enquiry.

A contact form doesn't solve it. It asks the visitor to do the work, then makes them wait. Plenty of people won't fill one in for a simple question. They'll just go back to the search results.

Check your own inbox before you buy anything

You don't need our word for whether you have this problem. Spend fifteen minutes with your last month of messages, emails, Facebook DMs and form enquiries:

  1. Write down every question a customer asked before they booked or bought.
  2. Tick the ones that are already answered somewhere on your website.
  3. Circle the ones that arrived after hours, and note how long your reply took.
  4. Count how many of the circled ones never turned into a job or a sale.

If most questions were already answered on your site, people aren't finding the answer, and a clearer page may fix that for nothing. If the same five questions keep arriving at night and going cold by morning, that's exactly the job a chatbot does well.

BEFORE YOU PICK ANY CHATBOT

What a site chatbot should do,
and what it never should.

Good jobs for a chatbot

  • Opening hours, location, parking and service areas
  • What you do and don't do, in your own words
  • Prices you already publish, quoted exactly
  • How to book, and what to bring or prepare
  • Taking a name, number and the details when it can't answer
  • Handing over to you when a conversation needs a person

Jobs it should never do alone

  • Make up a price or a quote that isn't in your material
  • Offer discounts, refunds or exceptions to your policies
  • Confirm a booking you haven't approved
  • Give medical, legal or financial advice
  • Answer from the wider internet instead of your business
  • Pretend to be a person when someone asks

Why the second list matters

In 2024 a Canadian tribunal ordered Air Canada to honour a refund its website chatbot had described, even though the airline's actual policy said otherwise. The airline argued it wasn't responsible for what the chatbot said. The tribunal disagreed: the business is responsible for all the information on its website, bot or not.

Case reported by the Law Society Journal (NSW). That's a Canadian ruling and we aren't lawyers, but the safe working assumption in Australia is the same: whatever the chatbot tells a customer, your business told them.

Six questions to ask any chatbot provider, including us

  1. Where do its answers come from, and can it answer from outside my material?
  2. What does it say when it doesn't know?
  3. Can it offer a discount, refund or booking without me? How do I stop that?
  4. Can I read every conversation, and for how long?
  5. What happens to visitors' names and numbers, and who can see them?
  6. If I leave, what do I keep?

If a provider can't answer all six in plain English, keep looking.

QUESTIONS OWNERS ASK

Straight answers.

Does a small business website need a chatbot?

Not always. If visitors rarely ask questions, or the same three questions could be answered by a clear FAQ page, fix the page first. A chatbot earns its keep when people ask the same handful of questions at all hours, after hours enquiries go cold before you reply, or you answer the same messages by hand every week.

Where does the chatbot get its answers from?

From your own website pages, price list and FAQs, not the wider internet. If the answer is not in what you have given it, it says so, takes the visitor's details and passes the enquiry to you.

What if it tells a customer something wrong?

Assume whatever the chatbot says, your business said. That is why ours only answers from your own material, logs every conversation so you can read it, and holds anything that commits the business, such as a booking, a discount or a refund, until you approve it.
