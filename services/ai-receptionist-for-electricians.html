THE SPARKY'S PHONE PROBLEM

A quote call and a fault call
ring the same phone.

You can't answer with the power off and your hands in a switchboard, and you shouldn't. So the calls stack up. When you finally ring back, the first five minutes of every call are the same: who are you, where are you, what's the house like, what's actually happening. Then you find out half of them needed a different answer. The quote could have waited until Thursday. The no-power call was a street outage, and the network was already on it.

Meanwhile the caller with a tripping safety switch and a dead fridge didn't wait for your callback. And the homeowner who wanted three quotes for a switchboard upgrade got two from electricians who picked up, and stopped there.

Two different calls, two different problems. Fault calls lose you the job when nobody answers. Quote calls lose you the job when the callback is slow and starts from scratch. A plain message service takes a name and number for both and leaves the sorting to you, at the end of the day, when you're least in the mood for it.

Count your own before you buy anything

Ten minutes with your call log tells you whether this is your problem:

  1. Go back two weeks and list every missed call from a number not in your contacts.
  2. Next to each, write fault, quote, existing customer or junk.
  3. Mark the faults you didn't reach within an hour and the quotes you didn't ring back the same day.
  4. Put a rough value on the ones that went nowhere.

If it's a call or two a fortnight, a callback habit is cheaper than any service, ours included. If your quote list is going cold every week, or after-hours faults are ringing out, keep reading.

USEFUL WHETHER YOU BUY OR NOT

The call sheet that makes
every callback shorter.

Whoever answers your phone, an apprentice, your partner, a message service or software, needs the same short list written down. Most electricians carry it in their head. Here's a starting point. Change every line to suit the work you take on.

1. Ask every caller

  • Name, mobile, and the job address including suburb.
  • House, unit, or commercial? Owner, tenant or property manager? For strata, who signs off on the work.
  • Roughly how old is the place, and has the switchboard ever been upgraded?
  • How did they find you, and are they an existing customer?

2. Fault calls: work out how urgent it is

  • Is the whole house out, part of it, or one circuit?
  • Do the neighbours and streetlights have power? If the street is dark, it's likely a network outage, and the distributor is the first call. Ausgrid's outage guidance sets out the same first check.
  • Has the safety switch tripped? Have they tried resetting it once, and did it trip again?
  • Any burning smell, smoke, scorch marks, buzzing, or a switchboard that's hot to touch?
  • Has anyone had a tingle or a shock from a tap, appliance or fitting?
  • Is there water anywhere near the switchboard, or storm damage to the service line?

3. Quote calls: what you need to price it

  • What they want: switchboard upgrade, EV charger, extra power points, downlights, smoke alarms, ceiling fans, rewire.
  • For an EV charger: which car or charger, where it will go, and how far that is from the switchboard.
  • Can they send a photo of the switchboard and meter? A photo often tells you more than ten questions.
  • Is any of it on the supply side, such as moving the meter or a new service line? In NSW that's work for a Level 2 accredited service provider. If you're not one, it's a referral, not a booking.
  • When do they want it done, and are they getting other quotes?

4. What gets said while they wait

Write it word for word. Most electricians want a caller with smoke, fire or an injury told to call Triple Zero first, to keep away from a hot or smoking switchboard, and not to touch anything that's given a shock. Whatever your lines are, the receptionist reads your approved script, not its own advice.

Ausgrid guidance checked November 2026. This is general information, not electrical or safety advice for a specific property.

QUESTIONS ELECTRICIANS ASK

Straight answers.

What does an AI answering service for electricians do?

It answers every call to your business number, day or night, in a natural voice. It asks the questions you would ask, works out whether the call is an urgent fault or a quote request using rules you have written, books quote visits into slots you have approved, and texts you a transcript and summary after every call.

Will it give my customers electrical advice?

No. It reads the safety lines you approve, word for word, such as staying away from a hot or smoking switchboard and calling Triple Zero if there is a fire or someone has had a shock. It does not diagnose faults or tell anyone how to fix them.

Can it book an after-hours call-out without asking me?

Only if you have told it to. Most electricians set urgent faults to come straight to them as a summary, so you decide whether to go and what to charge. Quote visits and routine jobs can be booked into approved slots without you.
