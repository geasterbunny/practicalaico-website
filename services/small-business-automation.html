WHERE THE HOURS GO

The work gets done.
The follow-up doesn't.

Most owners are good at the job they started the business to do. What slips is everything around it. A quote goes out on Monday and nobody follows it up, so the customer goes with whoever rang them back. An invoice goes overdue and chasing it feels awkward, so it waits another week. A happy customer leaves a five-word review and it never gets a thank-you, which the next person reading your reviews notices.

Each of these takes two minutes. The trouble is there are forty of them a week, they arrive while you're busy, and they all need you to remember, open the right app, find the details and write something polite. That's the part automation is good at. The deciding is the part it shouldn't do, so we leave that with you.

The ten-minute admin audit

Before you pay anyone to automate anything, write down what you'd be automating. Grab a notepad and list every admin job you did last week. Then score each one with these four questions:

  1. Does it repeat? At least weekly, in roughly the same shape.
  2. Does it follow a rule you could say out loud? "If a quote's had no reply in five days, check in."
  3. Is it mostly writing? A message, a reminder, a reply, a summary.
  4. Is a mistake easy to fix? Because you'll see every draft first, a bad one costs you a tap, not a customer.

Four yeses: a strong candidate. Two or fewer: keep doing it yourself, or fix the process first. If your list only has a couple of strong candidates, a calendar reminder and a few saved text templates will get you most of the way for nothing. We'd rather you did that than paid us for a system you don't need.
