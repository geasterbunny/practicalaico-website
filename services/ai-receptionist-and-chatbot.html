TWO FRONT DOORS

Find out which door
is leaking first.

A small business usually has one person answering everything, and that person is also doing the work. So the phone rings out during a job, the website form gets a reply two days later, and the Messenger question about Saturday opening hours never gets one at all. Each of those was someone ready to buy, asking a simple question.

The fix depends on where your customers actually come from. Buying a chatbot when your leads all ring is money wasted, and the reverse is just as bad. So before you look at any product, ours included, spend fifteen minutes on this:

The front-door check

  1. Phone: open your call log, go back two weeks, and count missed calls from numbers you don't know.
  2. Website: count the form enquiries in the same fortnight, and how long each one waited for a reply.
  3. Messages: scroll your Facebook and Instagram inboxes and count the questions that sat for more than a day.
  4. Repeats: write down the five questions you answer most often. Hours, prices, areas, availability, how to book.

Whichever number is biggest is your leaking door. If all of them are small, you don't need either product yet: a faster callback habit and an FAQ section on your site will do more for less. If the repeat questions on your list are the same ones every week, that list becomes the starting script for whichever assistant you choose.

BEFORE YOU EMAIL

What to have ready.

The setup is quicker, and the assistant is better on day one, if you can pull together a few things first. None of it needs to be polished:

  • Your five most-asked questions and how you answer them
  • Your prices, or the ranges you're happy to quote out loud
  • Your hours, service areas and anything you don't do
  • The jobs you'd let it book on its own, and the ones that always come to you
  • Where you want summaries and enquiries sent: text, email or both

QUESTIONS OWNERS ASK

Straight answers.

What's the difference between an AI receptionist and a website chatbot?

The AI receptionist answers your phone in a natural voice, takes the caller's details and books jobs inside rules you set, then texts you a transcript and summary. The chatbot sits on your website or Facebook Messenger and answers typed questions about hours, prices and booking from your own information. Same idea, different door.

Do I need both?

Not usually at the start. If most of your enquiries arrive as calls, start with the phone assistant. If most arrive through your website or Messenger, start with the chatbot. Add the other later if the numbers say so.

Will it make things up about my business?

It's trained on your own pages, prices and FAQs, not the open internet. When a question falls outside what it knows, it takes the customer's details and passes the enquiry to you rather than guessing. Every conversation is logged so you can check what it said.
