HOW EACH ONE GOES WRONG

Compare the bad days,
not the brochures.

Most comparisons list strengths. You'll learn more from what happens when things go badly, because that's the call you'll hear about.

When a human service fails

  • The message goes stale. The operator did the job, but the note sits unread while you're busy. The caller has already rung someone else.
  • The detail gets lost. The operator is working from a script for a business they barely know. A wrong suburb or a vague job description comes back to you to sort out.
  • The bill creeps up. Allowances run out in your busy month, and that's when each extra call costs you more.

When an AI receptionist fails

  • It mishears. A bad line, wind on a site, a strong accent or an unusual street name can all trip it up. Addresses and names are worth checking in the transcript.
  • It answers with confidence and gets it wrong. If the prices or service areas it was trained on are out of date, it will repeat them. Someone has to keep that information current.
  • The caller wants a person. Some will hang up as soon as they realise. You need to know how each system handles "put me through to someone".
  • A connected system breaks. It relies on your phone setup and calendar. Ask what callers hear if one of those goes down.

The risk with people is mostly delay. The risk with software is mostly a wrong answer delivered smoothly. That's why rules and transcripts matter more than how human the voice sounds.

Sort your last 20 calls into three piles

  1. Bookable: the caller wanted a time, and a script could have got them one.
  2. Answerable: they wanted your hours, area, price range or whether you do a certain job.
  3. Needs a person: a complaint, a complicated job, someone worried, or anything that needed judgement.

If the first two piles are small, a person or a better callback routine will serve you well. If they make up most of your calls, and you're getting enough of them, AI is worth pricing.

WHICH ONE TO PICK

Three common situations,
three different answers.

PICK A PERSON

Low volume or sensitive calls

You get few calls, or your callers are often distressed, vulnerable or asking about something complicated. Also the right choice if you don't run an online calendar, since AI would have nothing to book into.

PICK AI

Plenty of routine, bookable calls

Quotes, service calls and appointments that follow a pattern, at a volume where per-call fees add up, and with an online calendar it can work from.

USE BOTH

Different calls at different hours

A person or your office in business hours, AI after hours. Or AI for routine calls with anything unusual passed to a person. Worth raising when you get quotes.

BEFORE YOU SIGN ANYTHING

Seven questions for
any provider.

These work for a human service or an AI one. Put them to us too.

  1. What does a month cost at my call volume, including GST, minimums and after-hours rates?
  2. What happens to the price if I go over my allowance in a busy month?
  3. How quickly does the message or summary reach me, and how?
  4. What happens when a caller asks for a real person?
  5. What do callers hear if your system or my calendar goes down?
  6. Who can read call records, where are they kept, and how are callers told about recording?
  7. Can I hear it on a real call before I pay, and what's the notice period to leave?

Question six matters more than it looks. Rules on recording calls differ between Australian states, and recordings often count as personal information. One law firm's guide recommends telling every caller before recording starts. That's general information, so get advice for your own business.

QUESTIONS PEOPLE ASK

Straight answers.

What is the difference between an AI receptionist and an answering service?

An answering service is people, usually taking calls for many businesses and passing you a message. An AI receptionist is software that answers in a natural voice and, if it is connected to your calendar, can book the job during the call. One hands you a message. The other can hand you a booking.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than an answering service?

Not at low volume. At typical per-call rates, a human service usually costs less until you pass roughly 50 calls a month, and often well beyond that. AI costs more up front, and it starts to make sense when volume is higher or when booking the job during the call is worth more than a message.

When is a human answering service the better choice?

When you get few calls, when callers are often upset, confused or vulnerable, when calls rarely follow a pattern, or when you need someone to use judgement. It also suits a business with no online calendar, because an AI receptionist has nothing to book into.

Will callers hang up on an AI receptionist?

Some will, because some people simply want a person. We have no reliable figure for how many, and you should be wary of anyone quoting one without a source. Read the transcripts from your first few weeks. They will show how your own callers react.

Can I use an AI receptionist and an answering service together?

Yes. A common split is people during business hours and AI after hours, or AI for routine calls with anything unusual passed to a person. Which split makes sense depends on when your calls come in and what callers want.
