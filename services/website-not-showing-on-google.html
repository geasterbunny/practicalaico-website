FIRST, WHICH PROBLEM IS IT?

"Not on Google" is
three different problems.

Work out which of these you have before you change anything. They look the same from outside and have different fixes.

  1. Google hasn't indexed you. Your pages aren't in Google at all, so rewording them won't help yet.
  2. Google has indexed you, but you don't rank. You're on page four for the searches that matter, which is the same as not being there.
  3. You're checking the wrong thing. The map listings at the top of a local search come from your Google Business Profile, not your website.

The two-minute test

Open Google and type site: followed by your domain, with no space, for example site:yourbusiness.com.au. Then look at what comes back.

  • Nothing at all: you have problem one. Go through the checks below in order.
  • Some pages but not others: Google has found you but is filing some pages away. The Search Console reports below will tell you which and why.
  • All your pages: you're indexed. Skip to check six, which is about ranking.

Google itself notes that a site: search doesn't necessarily show every indexed page, so treat it as a quick read, not a final count.

SIX CAUSES, IN THE ORDER TO CHECK THEM

Most invisible sites have
one of these six problems.

1. Google hasn't found it yet

A brand new site with no links pointing at it can sit unnoticed for weeks. Google says it can take a few weeks to notice a new site. Check: is your site added to Google Search Console? If you don't know, it probably isn't. Adding it and submitting your sitemap is the most useful thing on this page. Then link to your site from your Google Business Profile and your social bios.

2. Something is telling Google to stay away

Sites are often built with a "don't index me" switch turned on so the half-finished version doesn't appear in search. Sometimes nobody turns it off at launch. Check: on WordPress, go to Settings, then Reading, and make sure "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is unticked. On any site, open yourdomain/robots.txt in a browser. If you see Disallow: / on its own under "User-agent: *", that line blocks the whole site. In Search Console, the URL Inspection tool will tell you if a page carries a noindex tag.

3. A redesign changed your web addresses

This one catches established businesses. A new site goes live, the page addresses change (/services.html becomes /what-we-do/), and nobody redirects the old addresses to the new ones. Google keeps sending people to pages that no longer exist, and your rankings quietly fall away. Check: search site:yourdomain, click a few of the results. If any land on a "page not found", you have this problem.

4. The sitemap is missing, stale or full of junk

Your sitemap is the list of pages you're asking Google to index. If it lists pages deleted years ago, or thousands of auto-generated pages, Google spends its time on those instead of the ones that matter. Check: open yourdomain/sitemap.xml. Does it load? Do the addresses in it match the pages you actually have? Then look at the Sitemaps report in Search Console for errors.

5. Pages that look empty or duplicated

Google won't index every page it finds. Pages with a couple of lines of text, near-identical suburb pages with the name swapped, and "not found" pages that tell Google everything is fine (a soft 404) are all likely to be crawled and then left out. Check: in the Page indexing report, look for "Crawled - currently not indexed", "Duplicate without user-selected canonical" and "Soft 404".

6. You're indexed, but not for the searches that matter

If your site comes up when you type your business name but not for "electrician Penrith", you're indexed and the problem is ranking. Usually it's one home page trying to cover every service and every suburb, or wording that doesn't match what customers type. Check: Search Console's Performance report shows the searches you already appear for. If none of them are services you sell, the pages need rewriting around those services, one page each.

QUESTIONS PEOPLE ASK

Straight answers.

How do I check if my website is on Google at all?

Type site:yourdomain.com.au into Google, with your own domain. If nothing comes back, Google has not indexed the site. If pages come back, the site is indexed and the problem is ranking, not visibility. For the full picture, set up Google Search Console and open the Page indexing report.

How long does it take a new website to show up on Google?

Google says it can take a few weeks to notice a new site or changes to an existing one. Submitting a sitemap in Search Console and getting a link from somewhere Google already visits, such as your Google Business Profile, helps it find you sooner.

My site shows when I search my business name but not for my services. Why?

That means Google has indexed you, so this is a ranking problem. Usually the site has one page trying to cover every service and suburb, or the words on it do not match what customers type. A page per main service, with the suburbs you work in written on it, is the usual fix.

Can I fix this myself?

Often, yes. A leftover noindex setting, a missing sitemap or a site that was never added to Search Console can each be fixed in an afternoon at no cost. An audit is worth paying for when the reports show hundreds of excluded pages, after a redesign changed your web addresses, or when you have done the basics and nothing has moved.
