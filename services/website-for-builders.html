SIXTY PHOTOS, NO STORY

A wall of pictures
isn't a portfolio.

Plenty of builders' sites have the same gallery page. Open it and there are dozens of finished kitchens, a deck, half a slab, somebody's ensuite and a photo of the ute. No captions. No suburbs. Nothing to say which photos belong to the same house.

You know the story behind each one. The kitchen used to be two poky rooms with a load-bearing wall between them. A visitor sees a nice white kitchen that could have come from a brochure.

That story is what they want. Your hardest problem, solved, can sell better than your prettiest photo.

The other cost: driving to the wrong jobs

Look back at your last ten site visits. How many were never going to happen, because the budget was half what the job needed, the owners were two years from starting, or it was work you don't do? Count the hours. If your form only asks for "name, email, message", every one of them arrives looking the same.

Before and afters that actually work

  • Stand in the same spot. Same corner, same height, same direction.
  • Take the before photo on measure day. Nobody remembers once the skip arrives.
  • Show one photo of the middle. Frame up, beam in, waterproofing done. The bits that get covered up show how you build.
  • Label a render as a render. If the house isn't built yet, say so under the picture.
  • Say what part was yours. If you took it to lock-up and someone else did the fit-out, say that.

Ask before you publish somebody's house

Get the owner's okay in writing, even by text, before their home goes on your site. Crop out house numbers, number plates and family photos. Use the suburb, never the street. If a professional took the photos, check your deal with them covers your website.

ENQUIRY QUALIFICATION

Seven questions that
save a wasted drive.

Make most of them taps, not typing, so it can be finished one-handed on the couch.

  1. What are you planning? Extension, whole-house renovation, kitchen or bathroom, granny flat, new build, something else.
  2. Which suburb is the property in? Not the full address yet.
  3. Do you own it? Owner, buying soon, or an apartment in a strata scheme. Strata work may need owners corporation approval, so you want to know early.
  4. Where are the plans up to? Just an idea, sketches, drawings done, approval lodged, approved.
  5. When would you like work to start? Next three months, this year, next year, no idea.
  6. What budget range have you set aside? Your own brackets, plus "not sure yet".
  7. Anything we should know? One open box, then name, phone and email.

Don't use the answers to ignore people. A polite "that's smaller than we take on, try a local handyman" costs you thirty seconds.

LICENCE, CONTRACT, COVER

What a careful owner
is checking for.

For residential work in NSW, these four are worth covering in plain words:

  • Licence. Residential building work over A$5,000 can't be advertised without a licence. Advertising, and the guidance names the internet, must show the licensee's name (or registered business name), the licence number with the right category of work, and a business phone number. Use the footer of every page.
  • Contract. Work over A$5,000 including GST needs a written contract, and the owner must get the Consumer Building Guide before entering into it.
  • Cover. Over A$20,000, the owner must be given a Home Building Compensation certificate, and where that cover is required you can't ask for a deposit until they have it.
  • Deposit. The most an owner can be asked for is 10 per cent.

Other states and territories have their own thresholds and schemes, so check with your regulator.

Sources: NSW Government, building trade advertisements and contracts for residential building work, checked November 2026. General information, not legal advice.

QUESTIONS BUILDERS ASK

Straight answers.

What should a builder's website include?

Your licence details, the kinds of jobs you take and the ones you don't, the areas you work in, four to six real projects each told as a short story, how you work from first call to handover, and an enquiry form that asks about scope, suburb, timing and budget. Five pages is enough for most builders and renovators.

How many projects do I need in my gallery?

Fewer than you think. Four to six projects with a before photo, an after photo and a plain caption tell a homeowner more than sixty loose images. If you only have three jobs photographed properly, show three.

Do I have to put my builder licence number on my website?

In NSW, yes if you advertise residential building work that needs a licence. NSW Government guidance says the licensee's name (or registered business name) and licence number must be shown in all advertising, and it lists the internet. Other states and territories have their own rules, so check with your regulator.

Should I ask for a budget on the enquiry form?

Yes, as a range with a 'not sure yet' option. You can spot a mismatch before you drive out, and people who are early in their planning won't give up on the form.
