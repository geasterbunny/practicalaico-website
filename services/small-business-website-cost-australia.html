WHAT MOVES THE PRICE

Six things that turn
A$2,000 into A$15,000.

When two quotes are miles apart, it's almost always one of these. Knowing them lets you decide what you actually need before anyone prices it.

  1. Number of pages. Home, services, about, work and contact covers most local businesses. Every extra service or suburb page adds writing, design and checking.
  2. Who writes the words. If you supply the copy and photos, it's cheaper. If the builder writes it from scratch, you're paying for a copywriter too.
  3. Template or custom design. A template is quicker. A custom design costs more and only earns it if your brand needs to stand apart.
  4. Things it has to do. Online bookings, payments, a members area or a product catalogue each add real build time.
  5. The platform. Some sites run on a page builder with a stack of plugins. Each plugin can need updates, which is part of why care plans exist.
  6. What's hiding in the monthly fee. Some quotes look cheap because the real money is in a monthly plan with a minimum term.
THE THREE-YEAR SUM

Compare quotes on
three years, not day one.

The up-front price is the number everyone compares. The monthly fee is the one that costs you. Run this on every quote you get, and it takes two minutes.

  • Write down the build price, and whether it includes GST
  • Add every monthly fee (hosting, care plan, subscription) and multiply by 36
  • Add the domain renewal for three years
  • Add anything they charge for changes, if you'll need them
  • Then ask: if I leave in year two, what do I walk away with?

A worked example, using the guide ranges above. Say the build is A$3,000, with A$50 a month hosting and a A$100 a month care plan comes to A$3,000 plus A$5,400 over three years: A$8,400, before any changes. A DIY builder at A$40 a month is A$1,440 over the same three years, plus your evenings. Neither is wrong. They're just very different numbers from the ones on the first page of the quote.

QUESTIONS OWNERS ASK

Straight answers.

How much does a small business website cost in Australia?

One independent Australian cost guide puts most small business sites at typically A$2,000 to A$6,000, with custom builds at A$6,000 to A$15,000. An agency guide puts them higher, at A$3,000 to A$10,000. Then add the monthly costs: hosting, a domain, and often a care plan. Our Website Build is from A$1,490 fixed, with hosting at cost.

Why do quotes for the same website vary so much?

Because the quotes are rarely for the same thing. The number of pages, who writes the words, custom design versus a template, online payments or bookings, and what is bundled into the monthly fee all move the price. Ask each builder to list exactly what is included before you compare.

What are the ongoing costs after a website launches?

Hosting, the domain renewal, and often a care or maintenance plan. One Australian guide puts total ongoing costs at typically A$75 to A$400 a month. A site with no plugins needs less upkeep, which is why ours has none and is hosted at cost.

Is a DIY website builder cheaper?

Up front, yes. Builder subscriptions typically run A$30 to A$60 a month. Over three years that adds up to roughly A$1,080 to A$2,160, plus your own evenings, and the site lives on their platform. If you enjoy doing it and have the time, it is a fair choice.
