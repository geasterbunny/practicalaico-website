WHY QUOTES GO COLD

Silence usually isn't a no.
It's a maybe that nobody chased.

When a quote goes quiet it's tempting to read it as "too expensive". Often it's something duller. The customer opened it on their phone in the car park and forgot. They're waiting on their partner to look at it. They got three quotes and yours is the one they haven't had a question answered on. Or it's sitting under forty other emails.

Whoever checks in, politely, with something useful to say, gives the customer an easy reason to ring back. That isn't always the lowest price. It's the one that felt looked after.

The reason most owners don't chase isn't laziness. It's that following up is a small job with no deadline, so it loses to every job that has one. By the time you remember, it's been three weeks and a follow-up feels awkward, so you skip it.

Run a ten-minute quote audit first

Before you pay anyone to fix this, check whether you've got the problem. Open whatever you quote from and look at the last two months:

  1. Count the quotes you sent.
  2. Count the ones that got a clear yes or no.
  3. Of the rest, count how many you followed up even once.
  4. Add up what the unchased ones were worth, then ask yourself honestly how many you'd have won with one phone call.

If you send a handful of quotes a month and chase them all, you don't need automation. A reminder in your calendar will do. If you're sending dozens and most of the quiet ones never hear from you again, that's money sitting on the table, and it's the easiest money in the business to pick up.

A FOLLOW-UP SCHEDULE THAT WORKS

Four touches over two weeks.
Use it even if you never hire us.

The common advice from quoting software makers is to wait two to three days before the first follow-up and to stop after a few tries. Here's a schedule built on that, with what each message should actually say:

  1. Day 0, with the quote: put a clear expiry on it, say 14 days. It gives the customer a reason to decide and gives you a natural reason to check in later.
  2. Day 2 or 3, "did it land?": a short text or email. "Just checking the quote for the deck came through okay. Happy to walk you through any of it." No pressure, just making sure it wasn't lost.
  3. Day 7, something useful: answer the question they probably have. Explain the difference between your two options, mention when you could start, or offer to split the job into stages. Give them a reason to reply.
  4. A day or two before expiry, the honest deadline: "Your quote's good until Friday. After that I'll need to re-price for materials." Only say that if it's true.
  5. After expiry, close the loop: "I'll close this one off for now. If anything changes, just reply here." It leaves the door open, and the ones who don't reply at least remember you were decent about it.

Change the timing to suit the job. A blocked-drain quote needs chasing the same day. A kitchen renovation might need a month and a site meeting. The rule that matters is that every quote gets the same treatment, not just the ones you happen to remember.

QUESTIONS OWNERS ASK

Straight answers.

How soon should I follow up on a quote?

Two to three days after it goes out is the common advice, long enough that you are not hovering and short enough that they have not already said yes to someone else. After that, a second nudge around day seven and a last one a day or two before the quote expires is plenty.

Won't automated follow-ups make me look like a pushy salesman?

Only if every message is the same canned chase. Each of our follow-ups is drafted from the actual quote, so it can mention the job, the options and the expiry date, and you read it before it sends. Three or four short, useful messages over two weeks read as organised, not pushy.

My job management app already has automatic follow-ups. Do I need this?

Probably not for quotes alone. If your app can send follow-ups and you are happy for them to go without you reading them, turn that on first. It is the cheaper answer. We are worth it when you want to approve each message, when your quotes live in more than one place, or when you also want invoice chasers, review replies and reminders handled the same way.
