WHERE THE WORK COMES FROM

Even referrals
look you up first.

A customer gets your name from their neighbour. Before they ring, they type it into Google. What they find in the next ten seconds decides whether they call you or the next name they were given.

If they find a Facebook page last posted in 2023, a broken link, or nothing at all, you've lost a job you'd already half-won. If they find a clean page with your recent work, the suburbs you cover and a button that rings you, they call.

The other half is people who've never heard of you. They search "bricklayer Penrith" or "plumber near me" on a phone. Google leans heavily on your Google Business Profile and on a website that clearly says what you do and where. A site is what that profile points to, and what Google reads to work out whether you're a match.

A two-minute self-test

Do this on your phone, not your computer, because that's where your customers are:

  1. Search your business name. Does your own site come up first, or a directory listing?
  2. Search your trade plus your main suburb. Are you anywhere on the first screen?
  3. Open your site, if you have one. Can you ring yourself with one tap in under five seconds?
  4. Is there a photo of a job you did in the last six months?

Four yeses and you're probably fine: spend your money elsewhere. Two or more noes and you're leaking work you never see.

WHAT A TRADIE SITE NEEDS

The must-haves.
Use this list with anyone.

Whoever builds your site, including you on a weekend, check it against this. None of it is fancy. All of it matters more than the design.

  • A tap-to-call button and a short job form, visible without scrolling on a phone
  • What you do, in the words customers search, on the first screen
  • The suburbs or areas you actually cover, written out
  • Photos of your own jobs, not stock pictures of someone else's
  • Your licence number and the licensee or business name, where your trade needs one
  • Loads fast on mobile data, not just on the office wi-fi
  • Linked to your Google Business Profile, with a sitemap and Search Console set up
  • Domain registered in your name, so you own it if you change builders

On the licence point: in NSW, building trade advertising, including on the internet, must show the licensee's name (or registered business name) and licence number for the right category of work. NSW Government guidance on building trade advertisements has the detail. Showing it also tells a nervous customer you're legit.

What you can skip

  • A blog you'll never write. An empty news page from two years ago looks worse than none.
  • Twenty pages. Five good ones beat twenty thin ones. Home, services, about, work and contact covers most trades.
  • Sliders, videos that autoplay, and pop-ups. They slow the page on a phone and get in the way of the call button.
  • A pile of plugins. Every one is something to update and something that can break.
